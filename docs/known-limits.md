# Known limits, by design

Not tasks: how the game behaves on purpose, and what it would take to change it.

- **Paint order where strokes overlap** differs per client: each applies its own ops at once and others' when their figure gets there, and blending isn't commutative. Measured: 97 of 290 overlapping texels differed; a player who joins later sees the server's order. Everyone matches again after a reload or rejoin. Weighed and dropped: the server re-sending canonical tiles where ops overlapped, a confirmed copy per surface with rollback (2× paint memory), a per-texel last-writer register (loses layering and fades), additive or max blending (changes the look).
- **Snapshot time is float32 seconds:** 2 ms steps after 8 hours of a session.
- **TCP head-of-line blocking:** one lost packet holds the ones behind it; smoothing and the adaptive delay hide it. WebTransport datagrams (Chromium) would remove it, if it ever shows.
- **Props turn in quarter turns only** (BL-022, rejected 2026-10-14). A prop's `rot` is a whole number of quarter turns everywhere (level files, placement, the network's ladder message), and the player's colliders are boxes aligned to the world's axes (`THREE.Box3`). A stepladder turned 7.5° or 45° fits no such box: the player would bump into air beside it, and climbing a turned ladder isn't supported. Turning it in small steps would need colliders, climbing and placement rebuilt for free rotation: not worth it.

## Multiplayer

How sessions work on purpose (decided 2026-10-04 to 10-08; details in `server/`, `src/net/protocol.ts`, `src/op-wire.ts`).

- **The server holds the paint.** One Node process hands out 5-letter codes, puts every paint op in one order, paints it into an in-memory copy of the session's paint and sends a joiner that paint as one file. So the snapshot is ready at once, SAVE is always at the session's detail, and a session doesn't die with the host's tab.
- **A private session lives in the server's memory only:** a restart or the last player leaving ends it, and the browser keeps no autosave. SAVE keeps the paint; a host can upload a save when starting. **Public sessions are kept on disk** (`DATA_DIR`): the admin's sessions come back after a restart with their paint, saved every 30 minutes, up to 5 saves kept by age; a release that changes the protocol, the surface table or the level wipes it.
- **PAINT DETAIL is picked once**, locked for everyone in the session: by the host of a private session, by the admin when a public one is made.
- **Ops are resolved dabs in face fractions and meters,** never texels or input replay, so the same op paints the same spot at any detail, and the painter paints with the values rounded as the bytes hold them, so every client and the server paint the same texels. Drips are decided by whoever painted and sent as ops; they trickle in real time, so clients can differ by dozens of texels.
- **Anyone can paint, scrub and move anything:** no ownership or undo; movement is client-authoritative and trusted; the server checks only that an op names a real surface and face (anti-cheat is BL-147). The admin can kick a player (they may rejoin at once: a kick is not a ban) and wipe a session's paint.
- **Weather, lightning, CCTV, neon flicker and fan phase are per player:** no shared clock; CCTV heads follow the local player. Nothing in play depends on them.
- **Players don't collide with each other.** Only a placed stepladder keeps off another player's box, checked by the placer's game. It also keeps out of an open door's way (0.9 m on each side of the wall), checked there too: a changed game could still put it there, and other ways it can block (a scaffolding's ladder, a narrow gap) aren't checked.
- **Pickups are personal:** each player collects their own; the server keeps what a player carried under their name to give it back when they return.
- **Ladders go with their owner:** a dropped player's stepladder stays for `SERVER.rejoinWindow` (3 min), then goes, and is never saved; a climber on a ladder that is moved just drops.
- **Thin paint is coarse at LOW:** a texel is 4 cm at 24 texels/m, so the thinnest marker line and drips are 4 cm wide; a save loaded at a lower detail is averaged down, at a higher one enlarged by nearest texel.
- **Rejected:** host-client (WebRTC) authority; an op log (unbounded, ~24 MB/h per sprayer); texture deltas on the wire (~400 KB/s at ULTRA); interest management (not needed for 40 players on one roof).
- **Only the admin makes sessions** (1.6.0): with no accounts the game can't tell the admin from a player, so a player's HOST is refused (`no-host`) unless the server runs with `PLAYER_HOST=1`. **No word filter** on session names or nicknames: moderation is by hand, on reports (the Terms).
- **Admin mode** (1.6.1, `server/admins.ts`, `src/admin`): an admin comes in from CCTV with a one-time pass, outside the players (no seat, stats, events or listing; one line in the server log as each comes and goes), unseen until they choose to be seen as a player named Admin. It beats bans and the limits per address, not the open links per address (`SERVER.linksPerIp`), counted before the JOIN. **It never reconnects:** any closed link ends it (LEAVE, a reload, a lost link, a wipe or restore). **One game tab per browser:** playing and administering at once takes another browser or profile. Kicks and bans stay on CCTV.
- **Locked faces** (admin mode's L): one face (a surface key and face rect) keeps its paint against every tool and run, on the server and in every game; a wipe or a restore clears the locks. **The lock race is accepted:** stamps a player makes as the lock arrives (about one ping's worth) stay visible only to them until they reload or rejoin.
- **A ban is by address and device, not by person:** everyone behind a banned address (a shared IPv4 behind a carrier's or a campus's NAT, a home's IPv6 /64) is kept out with the player, and a player with a new address and a cleared browser (a VPN, another device) gets back in. With no accounts there is nothing surer to ban by; the admin lifts a ban that caught the wrong people.
