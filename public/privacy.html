Privacy Policy

Last updated 2026-10-20

Hidden Roof is a small game made and run by George Gorchev (“I”, “me”). This page says what the game keeps and sends. The short version: I collect almost nothing, and what multiplayer needs is only held while you play.

What the game does not do

  • It sets no cookies.
  • It has no analytics, tracking or advertising, and loads nothing from other companies: the fonts, the code and the pictures all come from the game’s own site.
  • It has no accounts, so I do not ask for your email, your real name or a password.

Because of this, the game shows no cookie banner.

Single player: what stays on your device

Playing alone sends nothing about you to anyone, beyond your browser asking the game’s site for its files, as for any web page. The game keeps a few things in your browser’s own storage (localStorage), on your device only:

  • your settings: look, graphics, sound, and the keys you chose;
  • the nickname you last typed for multiplayer.

Your paint is not saved by the game. It lives in the page while it is open. If you press Save, the game gives you a file on your own device.

To remove what is stored, clear this site’s data in your browser’s settings (look for “site data” or “cookies and site data” for roof.hidden.haus). The game then starts fresh.

Multiplayer: what is sent, and who sees it

When you host or join a session, your browser opens a connection to the game’s server and sends:

  • the nickname you typed, and the session’s five-letter code;
  • where you stand and look, which tool and color you hold, and a few flags such as whether you crouch, about twenty times a second;
  • your paint, as it happens, and your stepladder;
  • if you host: the level, and the paint file you chose to start from, if any.

Other players in your session see your nickname, your figure, your paint and your stepladder. Anything you paint or type as a nickname is visible to them, so please keep personal information out of both.

The server sees your IP address, as any server you connect to does. It uses it to count connections and to apply per-address limits against abuse, and it writes it to its log (see below). It does not look you up or tie it to a person.

Your browser also keeps, for the tab you are in, a session key that lets a reload put you back into your session as the same player. It is kept in the tab’s sessionStorage and goes when the tab closes or when you leave the session.

What the server keeps, and for how long

  • In memory only: the session’s level and paint, each player’s nickname, the key mentioned above, what they carry, and the address they connected from. The server writes none of this to disk, and has no database.
  • When you drop out of a session, the server holds your place for 3 minutes in case you come back. Then you are removed. When the last player is gone, the session and its paint are deleted at once. The session’s code is remembered for an hour, so that anyone who tries it is told the session has ended; nothing else is kept with it.
  • Restarting the server ends every session and clears all of this.
  • The server’s log is a plain text record with one line per event: the time, the session code, the nickname, the IP address and what happened (joined, left, dropped, refused, an error). I use it to find faults and abuse. The hosting setup rotates the log, which discards old lines automatically; how long a line lives depends on how busy the server is and on that setup.
  • The web server that delivers the game’s pages may keep the usual access records of any website (your IP address, the time, the file asked for and your browser’s name), rotated in the same way.

I do not sell these records or share them with anyone, other than the company that hosts the server, which handles the traffic. I may use them to deal with abuse, or if the law requires it.

Your rights

You can ask me what I hold about you, ask me to correct or delete it, or ask me to remove paint or a nickname that you put in a session. The server holds nothing that names you except what you typed as a nickname and your IP address in its log, so please tell me the approximate time and the session code (if you know it) and your nickname, and I will look. Contact is below. If you live where the law gives you more rights than this page does, they stay yours.

Children

Hidden Roof is not directed at children under 13, and I do not knowingly collect their data. If you are a parent and think a child has used multiplayer, contact me and I will remove what I can.

Changes to this policy

I may change this policy as the game changes. The date at the top shows when it last changed.

Contact

Questions or requests: open an issue at the game’s GitHub repository (issues there are public, so leave out anything private and say you would like to talk elsewhere), or write in the Discord.
